# Plan for version 2

Canon was initially thought out to be a solution to standardize smaller atomic notes for project documentation.
The idea was borrowed from the spec driven design concept and thus carried a bit of it's mistakes (from my POV)
As processes matured the idea of merging canon with my own set of processes that i've been following became obvious.

And so i started the canon redesign process, using canon itself. The goal is to redesign the whole workflow and THEN (just then)
use Canon to design itself.

# Manifesto

## 0. Think outside the brain

The benefit was not using canon for documentation but rather using it to put design into words. Section 11.2 of the book "How to take smart notes" by Sohnke Ahrens describes Niklass Luhmann thought process:

> It is not possible to think systematically without writing

Also reinforced by Feynman when a visitor called the notebooks a record of his thinking:

> They are my thinking process. I actually did the work on the paper.

My understanding of this is that it also applies to typed words even though in a weaker less stimulant way (typing is faster than thinking while writing gives you time to think during the process). And thinking of co-authoring designs with LLMs they also benefit from written words... or in their "thought" media as we call it "tokens" or "context".

## 1. It's all a bunch of cycles

To write your thoughts **is to think**. Then by reading your thoughts you **think again** about it.
This creates a self feeding cycle that gives you the opportunity to re-think something every time you work on it.

## 2. Thoughts are living matter

If you just read your thoughts and leave it like that they will never evolve. You must read them with an always critical mind.
You challenge your own thoughts with the external world and your internal newer thoughts as well and by that it can only grow stronger... or be forgotten maybe. That's part of the work.

# How it applies to Canon

Canon was initially composed of the CLI, mainly. Now the plan is to have the CLI being pen that turns your thoughts into words on paper. In this case to bytes in a file.

1. It loses the ability to support multiple custom records,
2. SPEC record type gets deprecated, being replaced by `service` and `design` records.
   a. service records the problem that the you're proposing yourself to solve along with the persona that will use your solution and any constraint around it.
   b. design records the proposed solution to the problem and how it interacts with the world.
3. Records start having links between themselves so every time a design uses a word defined in the domain it should have a way to confirm the meaning and context of that word.
4. The most important part of canon starts being the SKILL and its surrounding processes.
   the skill is intentionally triggered only. I believe you should be conscious about the process all the time when you're proposing yourself to solve a problem.

## Why SPEC goes away

SPEC driven development comes from a early and young development process in the age of AI. It solved a problem for companies that were moving too fast and needed a way to record what was being added. Also added complexity to the process and a new layer to go stale.

### How does SDD compares to canon

A SPEC would require you to update it every time something changes. The SPEC is the living thing that then needs to be translated into code.
The SPEC is also both service AND design in a way. Even though it's more design than service. The proposed distinction is that by splitting the concepts into 4 layers you're able to work on it back to back and only change the aspects that need changing.
An ADR for example will record any conscious technical decision and why it was made. that's fuel for future decisions to be made consistently. That's also something that does not get updated all that often unless the developer chooses to do a major migration.
A design (or design specification... or SPEC if you prefer) is the closest one to a SPEC but the proposed usage is not to be the source of truth to a solution but rather be the workbench to which the solution is written and planned. You want to throw it away later? fine... you want to keep it? even better. That's also fuel for later decisions to be made more consistently.
A service definition then is the core definition of the proposed value being created. it will likely be defined at the root of many designs and if all goes well it shouldn't get touched again just like ADRs and just serve as reference to future decisions... It defines who you're working towards and what do you aim to solve. It also defines the scope of your problem to try to shield you from scope creeping.
And then, the most important of all: The domain definition. The domain defines the world building of the rest of your software design. It can be useful both during design AND during development. If we define on the domain that `a campaign is a tracked whatsapp flow that can be sent multiple times through sendouts.` you have the campaign defined & the next hook to also define what is a sendout. That also means that when you're about to write the data structures you have a reason to use the term `campaign` instead of any other terms because it is defined at the base world building rules of your design that the word CAMPAIGN means this.
